What the harness measured, and what it found

A retrieval-augmented system answering questions about SP 800-37r2, SP 800-53Ar5, SP 800-171Ar3, SP 800-78-5 and SP 800-30r1. Every number below is read back from a committed file — the judge's verdicts, the hand labels, and both library runs — so the page needs no model to say what it says.

Retrieval · did the search find the supporting text?

One retriever could not find what the questions asked for

Ten of the 26 answerable questions were served no part of their gold span in the top five. The misses collected on questions naming a document by its identifier, where an embedding blurs SP 800-78-5 into every neighbouring number. BM25 alone is worse overall; fused by reciprocal rank, better.

Four records improved, two got worse — one of the two had been fully covered and now is not. An average over 26 records hides that, which is why the per-record view is printed by compare_retrievers.py and not summarised away.

What these four words mean
hit@5 the share of questions where one of the five retrieved chunks contained the needed quote whole
covered@5 the share where the five chunks together held it, even split across two. Higher because it does not charge the retriever for where the chunk boundaries fell
coverage@5 the average fraction of the needed quote those five chunks held — partial credit rather than yes/no
MRR mean of 1/rank: the needed chunk first scores 1.00, second 0.50, fifth 0.20, not found 0. Rewards ranking, which hit@5 ignores

Refusal · did it decline when the corpus has no answer?

The judge against the hand labels, record by record

Twelve questions the corpus cannot answer. Declining is the only correct response, and an answer counts only when it declined and invented nothing. The labels are the ground truth; the two judges and the phrase-list tripwire are scored against them.

What the columns are
label: refused a human read the answer and decided whether it declined. This is the ground truth the other three columns are scored against
label: fabricated the same human, second question: does the answer assert anything the retrieved chunks do not support
phrase list the cheap detector: a list of phrasings like “is not in the context”. No model, no cost, and no opinion about fabrication at all
qwen3 / gemma4 the two LLM judges, each deciding both axes separately from the answer and the chunks. gemma4 also wrote the answers, which is why a second judge exists

The phrase list called the refusal axis correctly 12 out of 12 — better than either judge. It cannot express the second axis at all: an answer that declines and then invents a figure is an unconditional pass for it. That cell is empty on this fixture, so the replacement is kept for what it can see, not for beating what it replaced.

RAGAS · was the answer supported, and did it answer the question?

Fusing BM25 in moved the generation-side metrics too

RAGAS over the same 26 questions, before and after the retriever changed. Three metrics up, one down — context_recall carries the same two records the fusion lost.

What these four words mean
faithfulness of everything the answer asserts, how much the retrieved chunks actually support. The metric for invention
answer_relevancy does the answer address the question that was asked? A true statement about the wrong thing scores low
context_precision of the chunks retrieved, how many were needed, and were the needed ones ranked first
context_recall of the reference answer, how much the retrieved chunks cover. The LLM-judged cousin of coverage@5 above

All four are scored by a model reading the answer and the chunks, which is why the same four names mean measurably different things in two libraries — see the panel below.

RAGAS vs DeepEval · do two implementations of one metric agree?

Two libraries, one set of answers, and almost no agreement per record

RAGAS and DeepEval scoring identical answers against identical chunks with the same local model. Averaged, the two faithfulness scores sit within 0.1 of each other. Per record they correlate at 0.11: the disagreements cancel.

Points off the diagonal are where the libraries disagree. Three of the furthest are records where the generator declined: DeepEval scores “Answer is missing” a perfect 1.00 — nothing in it contradicts the chunks — while RAGAS gives the identical four words 0.00, 0.50 or 1.00 depending on whether its claim extractor found anything to check.

Traceability · need → requirement → test → evidence

What is claimed, and what holds each claim up

Four things a person wants from the system, then ten requirements written as claims about the system rather than descriptions of the code. The needs came last, and the order is the admission: until then the requirements traced upward to nothing. Six are gates — a test fails and the build is red. Four are measurements: a number produced and read by a person, because no acceptance threshold has been agreed for any metric here, and a threshold invented to keep this page green would be worse than none.

Every test named above is checked against the collected suite and every evidence path against the repository. A reference that has rotted fails the build rather than leaving this page looking complete — which is the only thing that separates a traceability matrix from a document about one.

Drift · how much the number moves when nothing changes

The width of the noise, before any threshold is set

The same metric, the same code, the same fixture — scored several times, each in a fresh process. Inside one process greedy decoding holds and the answers are identical; across processes the model is reloaded, the GPU/CPU layer split is re-decided, and decoding diverges from the first flipped argmax. Any acceptance bar has to clear this, or it fires on a model reload rather than on a regression.

Validation · whether a person finished the task

The only measurement here a machine cannot make

Every other number on this page asks whether the system does what was specified. This one asks whether the specified thing was worth having: eight tasks somebody actually has, put to the system, and a judgement per task about what came back. accepted means the answer was usable and the passage shown was enough to accept it without opening a document. declined — it said the corpus does not cover this, and that was right: a success for trustworthiness and a failure for usefulness at once, which is why it is not folded into either.

Eight tasks, judged by the person who built the system, can falsify a claim of usefulness — not certify one. Read the outcomes rather than the ratio, and note what is missing: the timing. The session's clock ran on past the answer and into the judging, so those seconds are an upper bound, not a comparison with doing the task by hand. Re-timing has to happen on tasks nobody has seen yet.

V-model · which levels of the V this project has

Where the levels are missing

The same claims placed on the V. Each specification level on the way down is paired with the level that verifies it on the way up, and the pairing is the point: a level whose right-hand side is empty is a claim nobody checks. This harness is verified thoroughly at the bottom, and validated at the top exactly once — by the session above.
